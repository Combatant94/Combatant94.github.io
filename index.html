Real data · 2025 · the 10 industries with the most insolvencies
Flagship

UK Business Distress Monitor

Which industries are really struggling, not just the ones with the most closures?

What I did
I took every company insolvency in England and Wales from 2016 to 2026, 220,650 records, and loaded them into a MySQL database. Before any analysis, I checked my totals against the government's published figures. All 44 months and all 272 industries matched exactly. Then I compared each industry's insolvencies with how many businesses it has, so big industries don't look worse just for being big.
What I found
Restaurants have the most insolvencies, but pubs and bars are the riskiest: 7th by count, 1st once you adjust for size. Residential care is the one to watch, with its rate more than tripling since 2019. And when I tried forecasting the next three months, a simple 12-month average beat three machine-learning models.

Real data · NHS England · Apr–Jul 2025
Healthcare

NHS Waiting Times

Where are NHS waiting lists under the most pressure?

What I did
NHS England publishes waiting-time data as huge monthly files: over 77 million rows for April to July 2025. I cleaned and summarised them in Python, built the database in SQL Server, and made a Power BI dashboard covering 477 hospital trusts.
What I caught
When I first ran the numbers, 77.8% of patients ended up in an "Other" group. That didn't look right, so I checked my grouping rule against all 104 waiting-time labels in the data. It only recognised 12 of them. The problem was my rule, not the NHS data, and I caught it before reporting anything.

Real model output · same property, different postcode
Property

London House Price Predictor

How much does the postcode alone change a London house price?

What I did
I used 157,603 London property sales, added useful details like distance to the centre and room sizes, and tested four types of regression model. Then I turned the best one into a simple tool: enter a property's details and it estimates the price.
What I found
The model explains about 84% of the variation in prices on sales it had never seen. Keeping everything else the same, the same flat is predicted to cost about 3.5 times more in EC3A (the City) than in N18 (Edmonton).

Real data · all 3,214 airports in the project
MSc research · Networks

Global Airline Network

Which airports matter most because of where they sit, not just how busy they are?

What I did
For my MSc research I built a map of world air travel: 3,214 airports joined by 36,907 unique routes, merged from 66,771 airline route records. I used several measures to score how central each airport is, and plotted the results on a world map.
What I caught
One measure said the most central airports on Earth were two tiny airstrips in Orkney, Papa Westray and Westray, just 2.8 km apart. I recalculated the distances myself and showed it was a quirk of how that measure treats very short flights, not a real hub. The genuine hubs are places like Amsterdam, Frankfurt and Paris.
